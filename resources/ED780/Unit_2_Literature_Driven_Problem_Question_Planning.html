Assignment Details

Literature-Driven Problem Question Planning

Strong applied research topics are not developed from personal interest alone. They must be grounded in a real educational problem or opportunity, be connected to an applied setting, be supported by scholarly literature, and be focused enough to guide future research. This assignment will help you begin asking the questions that should shape your Chapter 2 Literature Review.

Assignment Directions Submission Details

In Unit 1, you used AI-supported brainstorming and a feasibility chart to generate and compare possible applied research project topics. In this assignment, you will select the topic that seems most viable and what the literature must help you understand before the topic can move toward preliminary readiness or approval.

You are not submitting your topic for approval in this unit. Instead, you are preparing for the Unit 3 Preliminary Topic Readiness Check by developing literature-driven problem questions and an early source search plan. This work will help you determine whether your topic has enough scholarly and practical grounding to continue.

Step 1: Selected Topic From Unit 1 Feasibility Chart 

Select the topic from your Unit 1 feasibility chart that appears most viable at this point. Provide a brief intro to the topic.

Step 2: Literature-Driven Problem Questions

Use the following questions to guide your literature review development. These questions should help you determine what you need to learn before the topic can move toward preliminary approval. These are not intended to be answered one by one, but they can help guide your review.

  • What does the literature say about the problem?
    • What do scholars and practitioners already know about this issue, challenge, or opportunity?
  • What are the historical aspects of the problem?
    • What background, policy, organizational, instructional, social, or educational history may help explain how this problem developed?
  • How has the problem evolved over time?
    • How has the problem changed because of new practices, technologies, policies, stakeholder expectations, leadership decisions, or educational conditions?
  • What are the possible causes or contributing factors?
    • What does the literature need to reveal about why this problem exists, persists, or remains difficult to address?
  • What prior solutions, interventions, strategies, or innovations have been attempted?
    • What programs, practices, tools, policies, models, or leadership strategies have already been used to address the problem?
  • What does the literature suggest may help address the problem?
    • What evidence-based practices, promising approaches, leadership strategies, or innovation-focused responses appear to be relevant?
  • Are there any gaps, limitations, or unresolved needs that may remain?
    • What may still be unknown, underdeveloped, inconsistent, or insufficiently addressed in the literature?
Step 3: Applied Research Connection

Applied research must connect scholarly inquiry to a real educational or organizational problem. Use the following questions to guide your response:

  • How does this problem appear in a real educational or organizational setting?
    • Explain what the problem may look like in practice.
  • Who is affected by this problem in practice?
    • Identify the stakeholders most connected to the problem.
  • What practical evidence might show that the problem exists?
    • Consider possible evidence such as participation trends, retention data, feedback, survey results, performance data, program records, attendance, completion rates, policy changes, or documented stakeholder concerns.
  • Why does this problem matter for educational improvement?
    • Explain how addressing the problem may improve teaching, learning, leadership, programming, stakeholder experience, organizational practice, or system effectiveness.
  • How does the topic connect to innovation, intrapreneurship, leadership, or system improvement?
    • Explain whether the topic involves a new process, service, program, tool, practice, leadership approach, or improvement effort within an existing educational system.
Step 4: Early Source Search Plan

Create a brief search plan that will help you begin locating sources for Chapter 2.

Include the following:

  • 8–10 search terms or phrases
  • 2–3 databases or search locations you plan to use
  • Types of sources you will prioritize
  • Types of sources you will avoid or use cautiously
  • Any questions you still have about whether this topic is viable
Step 5: Preliminary Topic Questions for Feedback

End your assignment by listing 2–3 questions you would like feedback on before moving into the Unit 3 Preliminary Topic Readiness Check.
